Research Interpretation:

The spacecraft has one modeled primary energy source: the Fission Nuclear Reactor. The reactor produces thermal power. After the assumed conversion efficiency, the resulting useful converted reactor power is allocated to propulsion, spacecraft systems, and the hypothetical curvature-control requirement. The simulator does not add a second independent nuclear-power source.

The tensor engine uses local physical coordinates measured in meters, so the numerical spatial derivatives have inverse-length units. The Lorentzian-signature diagnostic includes the off-diagonal time-space metric component g01 = g10. For the metric structure used by this simulator, the time-x block must have a negative determinant, while the y and z spatial terms remain positive. The full-region result is obtained by sampling the modeled curvature-control region. The program does not change the test merely to obtain a PASS.

The transport relationship Q(h)=1/(1−h) is a deliberately defined trial ansatz used to connect the one-year mission target to a metric parameter. It is not a result derived from established general relativity. The Einstein equations calculate the source tensor associated with the selected trial geometry. They do not establish that a physical fission reactor can convert its output into the required controlled spacetime curvature. The tensor and signature diagnostics presently use the numerical demonstration amplitude TENSOR_AMPLITUDE. The one-year transport requirement H_REQUIRED is calculated separately. A PASS for the demonstration metric therefore does not by itself establish that the one-year target geometry is physically realizable.
