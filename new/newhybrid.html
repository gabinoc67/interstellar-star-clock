Assumptions, equations and reference data

Steady versus frozen: Setpoints can remain constant while a system operates. Power follows calculated demand; stored energies stop rising when charging balances losses. No random fluctuation is added.

Flight: Fixed 54.6-million-km corridor, Earth/Mars point masses, 12 km/s departure. Controller requests 0.0001 m/s², limited by engine capacity; braking starts halfway. Solar gravity, moving orbits, launch and landing are omitted. Endpoints are approximate and arrival may be a flyby.

Power: Reactor thermal limit 10,000 MW, conversion 40%, engine electric limit 3,200 MW, field/layer limit 600 MW, systems limit 200 MW. Jet efficiency 60%. Reactor demand follows electrical loads. Field energy loss coefficient 0.001/s and habitat loss 50 kW are engineering assumptions. The energy residual compares source energy with storage, heat, exhaust and escaping layer energy.

Cabin: Fixed 1,000 m³ and sealed air mass; starts at 295.15 K, thermostat target 293.15 K, heat capacity 10⁷ J/K, controller gain 20,000 W/K, cooler COP 3. Pressure follows p = mRT/(MV). Thermal-control readings are illustrative, not a hull or life-support certification.

Layer: Charge-neutral ionized hydrogen shell, radii 150–300 m; supply target 10¹² pairs/s times local/initial solar-wind density; residence time 1 s, temperature 100,000 K. N follows dN/dt = supply − N/τ. Density = N/volume; pressure = 2nkT; plasma frequency and beta update. Residence time is assumed, so low beta does not prove confinement. Layer source consumption is tiny and omitted from flight mass.

Environment: Solar-wind baseline 5 protons/cm³, 400 km/s, density falling as 1/r² along an assumed radial route from 1 AU. Voyager readings are historical comparisons, not local telemetry.

Gravity: Matter-shell gravity uses proton/electron mass and particle energy/c² in a Newtonian spherical-shell approximation. Interior shell force is zero; exterior radial tides stretch while transverse tides compress. Field-energy gravity is displayed only as GM/R² scale, not as a solved dipole gravitational field. A one-second radial tidal strain is not spacetime expansion. Full electromagnetic stresses, supporting equipment and Einstein–Maxwell geometry are not solved; no gravity-based propulsion benefit is applied.

Neutral Lorentz force: q(E + v × B) = 0 for q = 0. Polarization, collisions, ionization and neutron magnetic moments are separate effects. Neither magnetic rock protection nor a neutron population is assumed.

Whale/submarine: Hull, insulation and tanks manage pressure, heat and balance. Ordinary vacuum buoyancy is 0 N. Rotation supplies apparent crew gravity.

Casimir: Ideal parallel perfect conducting plates at zero temperature, negligible edge effects. E = −π²ℏcA/(720a³), u = E/(Aa), P = −π²ℏc/(240a⁴), F = PA. Negative energy is relative to the reference vacuum. E/c² is this energy contribution only, not total apparatus mass or a solved gravitational field. Controls prescribe quasi-static plate motion. An ideal actuator balances attraction; its power into Casimir energy is dE/dt = −F da/dt. Over a complete reversible cycle the net work is zero. Plate inertia, actuator inefficiency, finite conductivity, roughness and thermal corrections are omitted. Laboratory time advances by one second per real playback second, separately from mission time. The ideal mechanical exchange is shown separately and does not change mission mass, propulsion or reactor accounting; no actuator efficiency model is assumed. A constant energy offset is not ongoing power or reusable fuel. Theory discussion; 1997 measurement.

Historical references: Voyager 1: 0.08/cm³ (2013 report); Voyager 2/Cassini: ~0.12 neutral H/cm³ and ~0.5 nT upstream reference (2019); Voyager 2 plasma instrument shutdown (2024); NASA solar-wind reference. Voyager values are comparisons, not Earth–Mars measurements.
