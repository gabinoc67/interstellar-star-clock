Gravity around the ship · applied to flight

Front points toward Mars. Positions sample the same Earth–Mars gravitational field used for acceleration. The six hull readings are field magnitudes; the center reading also shows its signed direction. Percentages use Earth standard gravity, 9.80665 m/s².

Flight model: a fixed, straight Earth–Mars corridor, 54.6 million km between endpoints, both 400 km above their planet surfaces. Earth and Mars are point masses. Their gravity enters the velocity-Verlet flight integration. Solar gravity, moving planetary orbits, atmosphere, launch, and landing are outside this corridor model. This is simulated telemetry, not live planetary ephemeris.

Departure: initial outward speed is 12 km/s; its kinetic energy is displayed separately. The controller accelerates over the first half of the corridor and brakes over the second. Reaching the Mars endpoint at speed is recorded as a flyby. The target of 20% light speed remains a comparison reading.

Protection: the displayed percentage is the reduction in modeled frontal proton impacts relative to identical field-off rays. A dipole surrounds the hull. Two launch apertures include particles outside the hull footprint; unresolved paths count as impacts. Test conditions and counts are displayed. Neutral-rock protection from this magnetic mechanism is 0%. The dipole has polar openings, so the frontal result is not assigned to every direction.

Crew 1g: electrical motors spin a 100 m radius habitat. Its apparent gravity is ω²r. This does not create a gravitational field outside the ship. Spin-up energy and continuing mechanical losses are charged to the systems allocation.

Energy: 40% of 10,000 MW thermal becomes electricity: 80% propulsion, 15% magnetic systems, 5% ship systems. Jet efficiency is 60%. Field energy is the integrated exterior dipole energy; internal coil energy and engineering losses are omitted. The 12 MV electrical pulse uses voltage × current × duration, not voltage alone. A pulse is an electrical diagnostic, not a lightning shield in vacuum.

Clocks: UTC and Central time use your device clock. TAI is estimated as UTC + 37 seconds for the October 2026 setting; update that offset if leap-second rules change. The onboard Earth Central reference display matches the Earth display. Mission reference clocks advance with simulated Earth time and pause with the flight. The independent ship clock integrates proper time using the special-relativistic speed correction; gravitational clock corrections are omitted. Clock synchronization is a display convention, not an effect of inertia or a simulated communications link.
